Healthy weight loss generally involves creating a modest calorie deficit, meaning the body uses more calories than are consumed, achieved through a combination of diet and physical activity rather than diet alone.

Gradual weight loss, such as a slow and steady pace over time, is often recommended over rapid weight loss, since gradual approaches are more likely to be sustainable and more likely to preserve muscle mass.

Adequate protein intake during weight loss helps preserve muscle mass while losing fat. Sources include lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, and tofu.

Fiber-rich foods, such as vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and legumes, can support feelings of fullness and satisfaction, which may help with managing calorie intake without feeling overly restricted.

Whole, minimally processed foods tend to be less calorie-dense per volume than many ultra-processed foods, which can make it easier to feel satisfied while consuming fewer calories overall.

Strength training during weight loss helps preserve muscle mass, which supports metabolism and overall body composition, while aerobic exercise supports calorie expenditure and cardiovascular health.

Very restrictive diets or very low-calorie approaches are generally not recommended without medical supervision, since they can be difficult to sustain, may lead to nutrient deficiencies, and are often followed by regaining lost weight.

Behavioral strategies, such as planning meals ahead of time, eating slowly, and paying attention to hunger and fullness cues, can support sustainable habits during a weight loss process.

Weight loss is not always linear, and plateaus — periods where weight stays the same despite continued effort — are a normal part of the process for many people.

Sleep and stress management play a role in weight loss, since inadequate sleep and chronic stress can affect hunger hormones, cravings, and motivation for physical activity.

Weight loss goals and appropriate calorie targets vary significantly between individuals based on factors like starting weight, activity level, age, sex, and health conditions, so general guidelines should be adapted to the individual rather than applied uniformly.

Consulting a registered dietitian or healthcare provider is recommended for personalized weight loss guidance, particularly for individuals with underlying health conditions, a history of disordered eating, or those considering significant dietary changes.

Sustainable weight loss is generally supported by long-term lifestyle changes rather than short-term or extreme approaches, since habits that can be maintained over time are more likely to support lasting results.
